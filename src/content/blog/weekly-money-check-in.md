Budgets rarely fail because the maths was wrong. They fail because nobody looked at them after the second week. By the time the statement is checked at the end of the month, a small drift has become a decision about whether you can afford food and a train ticket in the same week.

A weekly check-in fixes that with an amount of effort small enough to survive a deadline week. Five minutes, once a week, in the same slot.

## Why weekly and not monthly

A month is long enough to drift a long way before you notice. A week is short enough that the correction is small: one fewer delivery, one cheaper shop, one journey avoided. You are not auditing your life; you are checking whether the plan still describes the next seven days.

Weekly also matches how money actually leaves an account. Most spending is small and frequent, and frequent spending is exactly what a monthly review is bad at reconstructing.

## The five steps

Do these in order and stop when the five minutes are up.

1. **Enter the week's transactions.** Every purchase since the last check, with the amount and what it was for. Use receipts if you kept them, your banking app if you did not. If you use UniBudget, this is the only step that takes real time, and it gets faster after a few weeks.
2. **Compare what is left with the days that are left.** Divide the remaining monthly budget by the number of days until your budget resets. That is your daily allowance for the rest of the month. If £120 remains and there are nine days left, you have about £13 a day — including the days you already know will be expensive.
3. **Scan for one recurring charge or surprise fee.** One glance, not a full investigation. If something looks unfamiliar, note it and check it properly after the five minutes are up.
4. **Move money that has a different job.** If this is the week your income arrives, make the savings transfer and the irregular-costs set-aside before you spend anything else. If not, skip this step.
5. **Write one sentence about next week.** "Two packed lunches before Thursday, because the gig is on Friday." A specific, small intention beats a general resolution.

## Attach it to something you already do

The check-in survives when it is attached to an existing routine: Sunday evening before you plan the week, Monday morning on the bus, or the moment you sit down to do laundry. Put it in your calendar with a reminder. The same slot each week matters more than which slot it is.

Do not wait for a quiet week to start. The quiet week will arrive in the holidays, by which point you have stopped.

## If you are already over

Being over budget is information, not a verdict.

- **Find the category that moved.** It is usually one, not all of them.
- **Choose one change for the remaining days.** Fewer deliveries, one fewer night out, a cheaper shop. Protect rent, food and travel to campus.
- **Adjust the plan rather than abandoning it.** Move the overspend against a flexible category for the rest of the month, or accept it and reduce the set-aside slightly this month.
- **Do not rebuild the whole system.** Starting a new spreadsheet on a Tuesday is a well-known way to avoid the actual decision.

## What to ignore

A five-minute routine only works if it stays five minutes.

- Not every purchase needs analysis. Small and unremarkable is fine.
- You do not need twenty categories. Four or five is enough for most students: rent and bills, food, travel, social, and everything else.
- Do not chase penny-level precision. A budget that is roughly right and used weekly beats an exact one that is never opened.
- Do not compare your spending to someone else's. Their income, rent and family situation are not yours.

## What good looks like after a term

At the end of a term, a working routine looks unremarkable:

- Your budget total is close to what you actually spent, within a reasonable margin.
- You know which irregular costs are coming and roughly when.
- The savings transfer happened in most months, not all of them.
- You notice a new recurring charge within a week rather than at the end of the year.

That is enough. The aim is not a perfect record of every pound. It is knowing, most weeks, whether the next seven days fit inside the plan you made.
